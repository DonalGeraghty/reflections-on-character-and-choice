# The Practised Life

A personal, evolving handbook about how to live a good life, developed through experience, reflection and learning.

This is a collection of my own principles, observations, examples and lessons—not a finished self-help book. The chapters are starting places for my writing, and the structure can change as the handbook develops.

The model is Marcus Aurelius's *Meditations*: personal notes to return to, reminders about how I want to live, and reflections on what experience has taught me. I am writing to myself. Entries can be brief, questioning or unfinished; they should help me put what I learn into practice.

## Starting themes

- **Think Clearly** — Critical thinking, Stoicism, perspective, emotional regulation and recognising what is within our control.
- **Act With Good Character** — Honesty, integrity, kindness, respect, fairness and behaviour towards others.
- **Look After Your Body** — Fitness, nutrition, sleep, recovery and long-term physical health.
- **Make Discipline Easier** — Habits, consistency, environment design, routines and self-control.
- **Enjoy Being Alive** — Happiness, gratitude, relationships, experiences, meaning and appreciating everyday life.

These themes are flexible guides rather than a fixed chapter plan.

## Adding to the handbook

Agents working on the handbook should follow [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) for the writing voice and how to develop personal notes.

Write in the chapter that fits best, or add a chapter when needed. A short observation, a concrete example or a lesson from experience is enough to begin. Revise ideas as experience changes them.

Use an impersonal, reflective voice: quiet observations followed by practical lessons, in direct, everyday language. Keep the writing grounded in my experiences without narrating them through "I", "you" or "they". Develop the lessons without turning them into lectures for an audience. Use *Meditations* as a model for reflection and self-examination, while keeping the experiences and words my own.

Keep the content personal. Do not add generic principles, invented experiences or inspirational quotes to fill empty chapters.

Each chapter and numbered reflection opens with a short, memorable phrase in a Stoic spirit, formatted as a Markdown blockquote. Original phrases remain unattributed; any quotation credited to a philosopher should have a verified source. These opening reminders set the tone for the reflections that follow.
