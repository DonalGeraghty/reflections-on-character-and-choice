# Working on The Practised Life

## Purpose

This repository is a personal, evolving guide to living well, built from the author's experiences, observations and reflections. Marcus Aurelius's *Meditations* is the model: notes worth returning to and lessons worth putting into practice. Write for the author's own use rather than for an audience seeking a polished self-help book.

## How to develop the author's notes

- Treat the author's informal messages as source material. Understand the underlying point, then express it clearly in fresh words rather than merely correcting spelling or transcribing it.
- When asked to expand, develop the implications and practical lessons in the observation. Substantial expansion is welcome when requested, but each passage should add something rather than repeat the same idea.
- Preserve the author's meaning and degree of certainty. A diet described as probably the most effective tried should remain a personal assessment, not become a proven best diet.
- Ground reflections in experiences the author has actually supplied. Do not invent memories, outcomes, motives, routines or beliefs to make an entry feel complete.
- Keep personal observations distinct from general factual claims. An experience of feeling fuller or less bloated should not become a universal nutrition rule or an unsupported medical explanation.
- Leave room for uncertainty and revision as experience changes. Empty chapters can remain empty until there is something personal to develop.

## Voice and form

- Use an impersonal, reflective voice: quiet observations followed by practical lessons, in plain, everyday language.
- Keep the substance personal without narrating the author as "I", addressing them as "you", or describing them as "he", "she" or "they". Pronouns referring naturally to foods or other subjects are fine.
- Impersonal does not mean mechanically passive. Use natural sentences such as "The pleasure is brief" or "Meals higher in fat and protein were filling", rather than stiff constructions such as "Satisfaction was experienced".
- Use *Meditations* as a model for reflection and self-examination. Keep the language contemporary and the ideas grounded in the author's own life. Avoid imitation of ancient diction and borrowed authority.
- Give every chapter one short, memorable opening phrase, placed as a Markdown blockquote immediately below its main heading. Prefer a Stoic or Stoic-style reminder that captures the chapter's guiding principle. Preserve an existing phrase that fits, and avoid repeating it solely to fill this position.
- Give every numbered reflection its own short Stoic or Stoic-style phrase as a Markdown blockquote immediately beneath the level-three heading, with a blank line before and after it. Follow with the explanatory paragraphs. Prefer the author's supplied phrase where it fits. Move a matching opening sentence into the blockquote rather than repeating it in the prose, and avoid duplicating the chapter's opening phrase. Use plain `> Phrase.` formatting consistent with the existing chapter openings.
- Original Stoic-style phrases are welcome; leave them unattributed. If using a quotation attributed to a philosopher, verify its wording and source. Never present an original phrase or loose paraphrase as an authentic quotation. The opening phrase is a deliberate convention, not a reason to fill the chapter with generic sayings or invented experiences; otherwise-empty chapters can contain just the title and phrase.
- Prefer direct observations, considered questions and practical reminders. Avoid lectures, generic advice, motivational filler, moralising and exaggerated declarations.
- Use short, numbered reflections, with connected paragraphs that develop a clear principle into practical meaning. Give each reflection a distinct purpose; do not force a fixed number of entries or a length target.
- Let each reflection find its own length. Avoid a repeated five-paragraph template, routine closing summaries and recurring abstract phrases where a direct sentence would do. Related principles can recur as reminders, but each entry should contribute a distinct application or insight.
- Read new material alongside existing entries. Keep the distinctions clear: privacy should allow honest learning, acceptance should leave room for action, and discipline should support a life with rest and enjoyment. Develop the author's meaning without turning a memorable maxim into an absolute factual claim.
- Use the Character and Integrity and Living with Intention chapters in `README.md` as the preferred style references. The author explicitly preferred these over the older nutrition wording. Lead with a clear principle, develop it naturally and use concrete examples where they help. Avoid repeated summaries of past experience and commentary about what belongs in the notes. Food and Nutrition has been revised to follow this approach.

## Visual design and readability

- The author welcomes restrained visual elements, including small touches of colour, because they break up long passages and make important distinctions easier to remember.
- Design for GitHub's README reader, especially the mobile app, and for both light and dark mode. Keep the manuscript readable as plain Markdown.
- Use GitHub-flavoured Markdown tables for genuine comparisons, especially when two experiences reveal opposing patterns or a shared lesson. Keep tables narrow and legible on phones; use prose instead for long cells.
- Use occasional GitHub callouts (`> [!NOTE]`, `> [!TIP]`) for an important connection or practical reminder, never in every reflection. They should supplement rather than repeat the prose.
- A sparing use of colour through familiar emojis (for example 🟢 and 🟠 for contrasting patterns) is welcome when their meaning is clear from accompanying text. Do not rely on colour alone to communicate a distinction.
- Images or diagrams may be added when they make an actual insight easier to understand. Prefer local, accessible assets with useful alt text, restrained colours and small file sizes; avoid decorative stock images, heavy banners, excessive iconography and remote image dependencies. Never invent personal scenes or experiences in illustrations.
- Use consistent spacing and horizontal rules between chapters. Preserve stable chapter and reflection headings so Markdown links continue to work.
- A visual element should clarify, orient or give the eye a purposeful pause. Do not force one into each reflection, and never let formatting overpower the reflective voice.
- When a new entry makes an existing table, callout or diagram inaccurate, update that visual in the same change.

## Editing the repository

- Keep the entire handbook in the single manuscript `README.md`. Use its level-one heading for the book title, level-two headings for Table of Contents and chapters, and level-three headings for numbered reflections with short descriptive titles. Number reflections within each chapter. Do not recreate separate chapter files.
- When asked to add or rewrite material, edit the relevant chapter in `README.md`. Keep the final chat response brief and link to the edited chapter.
- Use judgement for routine wording and organisation within the request. Ask for clarification when the meaning depends on missing personal information rather than filling the gap with invention.
- Keep the Table of Contents at the start of `README.md` updated in the same edit whenever a chapter or reflection is added, removed, renamed, reordered or renumbered. Include every chapter and its reflections in manuscript order, with reflections nested beneath their chapter. Match their heading text and verify that each Markdown anchor points to the correct heading.
- Keep chapter links in `README.md` and internal cross-references accurate when headings change. Food and Nutrition and Exercise remain separate chapters within the manuscript. Reflection links should use their full descriptive heading anchors, since reflection numbers repeat across chapters.
- Keep these instructions aligned with the author's later preferences. The latest agreed voice is impersonal and reflective; earlier experiments with first-person reminders and third-person narration were superseded.
